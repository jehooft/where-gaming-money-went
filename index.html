      This was made by an AI

      Everything here — the research, the data model, the code and this text — was produced by an AI system (Claude, by Anthropic) working from public sources. No human analyst has audited the figures.

      That means errors are possible, including confident-sounding ones. Language models can misremember a number, conflate two similar statistics, or state an estimate with more certainty than it deserves. This project tries hard to guard against that — every worldwide total is tied to a named source, the regional model is automatically re-checked against eleven independently published figures on every build, and the working is written down in research/RESEARCH-NOTES.md so you can follow it — but those are mitigations, not guarantees.

      How to use it responsibly. Treat this as a well-sourced sketch of the shape of the industry, not as a citable dataset. The broad strokes — the arcade peak and crash, the handheld collapse, mobile overtaking everything — are robust and agree with multiple independent sources. Individual band values, especially before 1985, for arcades after 1990, and for any single company in any single region, are estimates that could be materially wrong. If a specific number matters to you, check the primary source before using it.

      What is a measurement and what is a model. Worldwide segment totals are researched from published figures. Console and handheld shapes are derived from published shipment data. Regional splits, company-level regional mixes, and the split of a segment total between individual platforms are modelled — they are reasoned estimates constrained to add up to the researched totals. The Method & sources panel sets out exactly which is which.

      How this chart was built

      Made by an AI. This project was researched, modelled and written by an AI system, and has not been audited by a human analyst. Hallucinated or mis-stated figures are possible. .

      What is being counted. Worldwide consumer spending on video games: coin dropped into arcade machines, software bought at retail or digitally, DLC, subscriptions and in-app purchases. Business-to-business revenue — arcade cabinets sold to operators, engine licences, advertising, esports sponsorship — is excluded, because counting it alongside player spending would double-count the same dollar.

      Hardware is included, but only dedicated games hardware. Consoles, handhelds and VR headsets count; gaming PCs, graphics cards and phones do not, because those are general-purpose machines and their buyers are not only buying a games device. This is why the totals here run above Newzoo's widely-quoted headline: Newzoo counts content only. 2024 reads as roughly $207bn here versus their $182.7bn, and about $15bn of that gap is console and handheld hardware. It also means a console band spikes in its launch quarter, which is exactly the behaviour you want to see on a chart about hardware generations.

      How the bands are cut. The three levels are segment → company → platform, but "company" means something slightly different per segment, and it has to:

      • Console & handheld — by platform holder. A band is all money spent on that ecosystem, including games from other publishers. PlayStation 4 includes Call of Duty.
      • PC — by storefront. An EA game bought on Steam counts as Steam. This is the only way to slice PC without counting the same sale twice.
      • Mobile — by publisher, which is naturally exclusive since each game has one.
      • Arcade — by the manufacturer of the machine that took the coin.
      • VR / Cloud — by platform owner.

      Where the numbers come from. Annual segment totals are the spine and are researched directly: Play Meter and Vending Times arcade surveys for the 1970s–80s; NPD/Circana and Gaming Alexandria's compilation of contemporaneous trade estimates for US retail 1972–1999; Capcom, Sega Sammy and JAMMA filings for the Japanese arcade market; DFC Intelligence for PC; Newzoo, Sensor Tower and company filings from 2012 on. Every platform band is then rescaled so each segment-year sums exactly to those researched totals.

      Platform shapes are derived, not guessed. Console and handheld bands are computed from hardware shipment curves — lifetime unit totals are matters of record, and the yearly split follows published shipment data — plus software attach and a live-service multiplier that grows over time as DLC and microtransactions take over.

      What is modelled rather than measured. The worldwide segment totals are researched. The split of those totals between regions is modelled: shares are anchored on published regional revenue where it exists (Newzoo by region from 2021, CNG for China, ISFE for Europe) and reconstructed from unit-sales geography before that. Company-level regional mixes use affinity multipliers derived from published regional hardware splits — which is how the chart knows Xbox has no Japanese business. The build re-checks the modelled regional totals against eleven independently published figures on every run and reports the gap; all eleven currently agree within tolerance, most within 8%. Pre-1985 figures and arcade figures after 1990 remain the least certain numbers here.

      An earlier version of this chart offered a quarterly view. It has been removed. No public quarterly series exists for this industry before roughly 2010, and none at all for arcades or pre-1995 console markets, so every quarterly value was one fixed seasonality assumption repeated fifty-seven times. It produced a regular sawtooth that looked like a measurement and was not, which is a bad thing for a chart to do.

      Where this deliberately differs from other published versions. The best-known chart of this shape shows arcade revenue collapsing to nothing after the mid-1980s, which is true of the United States and not of Japan, where arcade operating revenue was still a multi-billion-dollar business through the 2010s. The arcade band here stays visible, restricted to the video-game share of arcade operating revenue.

      Inflation. "Real" figures use US CPI-U annual averages, rebased to 2025 dollars. 2026 is a forecast year in every segment.
