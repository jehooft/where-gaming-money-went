      This was made by an AI

      The research, the data pipeline, the code and this text were produced by an AI system (Claude, by Anthropic) working from public sources. No human analyst has audited it.

      Errors are possible, including confident-sounding ones. Language models can misremember a number, conflate two similar statistics, or state an estimate with more certainty than it deserves. This project guards against that where it can — every figure is tied to a named source, both datasets are automatically re-checked against independently published figures on every build, and the working is written down in research/ — but those are mitigations, not guarantees.

      The two tabs are not equally uncertain, and it matters which you are looking at.

      • Electricity is the more solid of the two. The generation numbers are ingested verbatim from published statistics (Our World in Data's compilation of Ember and the Energy Institute; Eurostat for the fuel-level detail). Nothing about the shape of those bands is estimated by this project. The only derived quantity is the carbon series, which is generation multiplied by a published emission factor.
      • Gaming revenue is substantially modelled. Worldwide segment totals are researched from published figures, but the split between individual platforms, and between regions, is reasoned estimation constrained to add up to those totals.

      How to use it responsibly. Treat this as a well-sourced sketch, not a citable dataset. Broad strokes are robust. Individual band values — especially pre-1985 gaming figures, arcade after 1990, and any single company in any single region — are estimates that could be materially wrong. If a specific number matters, check the primary source first.

      02 · Electricity, 1900–2025

      The generation figures are not modelled. They are ingested verbatim from Our World in Data's Electricity production by source dataset, which merges Ember's global electricity data, the Energy Institute Statistical Review, and historical estimates that carry the world series back to 1900. The build reads a trimmed copy committed in data/source/, so the numbers on screen can be traced line by line to the published file.

      Sub-fuel detail comes from Eurostat. Clicking coal opens it into lignite, other bituminous coal, sub-bituminous coal, coking coal and anthracite; oil opens into fuel oil, gas oil, petroleum coke and refinery gas; solar into photovoltaic and solar thermal. Those splits are real reported statistics (Eurostat nrg_bal_peh) — which is why they are available for the European Union and its member states and not for the World, the United States or China. No global agency publishes electricity generation broken down by coal rank, so rather than invent that split, the app simply does not offer it where it is not measured. Sub-fuel shares are applied to the parent fuel's total so the two sources reconcile exactly.

      The carbon view. Switching the measure from terawatt-hours to CO₂ multiplies each band by an emission factor. Two bases are offered because they answer different questions:

      • Lifecycle — IPCC AR5 Working Group III, Annex III, Table A.III.2 median values, read from the published PDF. Includes construction, fuel supply and decommissioning, so wind, solar, hydro and nuclear are small but non-zero. Coal 820, gas 490, solar 45, hydro 24, nuclear 12, wind 11 gCO₂e/kWh.
      • Burned — combustion only, which is what national greenhouse-gas inventories measure, and biogenic carbon counts as neutral so bioenergy is zero.

      An error this process caught. The first attempt used the IPCC's direct emissions column for the combustion basis and the modelled carbon intensity came out about 22% below Ember's published figure for essentially every country and year. That column describes a modern reference plant; the operating fleet is older and less efficient. Fitting the factors by least squares across 672 published country-year intensities gives coal 931, oil 773, gas 593 and bioenergy approximately zero gCO₂/kWh — values that sit inside independently published fleet averages, and a bioenergy result that independently confirms biogenic carbon is treated as neutral. With those, 81 of 88 checks now agree within 20% or 30 gCO₂/kWh. The seven that don't are grids dominated by combined heat and power or by hydro, where a single fleet-average factor is a poor fit; they are listed in the notes rather than tuned away.

      01 · Gaming revenue, 1970–2026

      What is counted. Worldwide consumer spending: coin dropped into arcade machines, software bought at retail or digitally, DLC, subscriptions and in-app purchases. Business-to-business revenue is excluded, because counting it alongside player spending would double-count the same dollar. Dedicated games hardware is included (consoles, handhelds, VR headsets); general-purpose hardware is not (gaming PCs, GPUs, phones). That is why totals here run above Newzoo's headline, which counts content only.

      How the bands are cut. Console and handheld by platform holder, so a band is all money spent on that ecosystem. PC by storefront — an EA game bought on Steam is Steam revenue — because that is the only division that does not count a sale twice. Mobile by publisher. Arcade by the manufacturer of the machine that took the coin.

      Sources. Play Meter and Vending Times arcade surveys for the 1970s–80s; NPD and Gaming Alexandria's compilation of contemporaneous trade estimates for US retail 1972–1999; Capcom, Sega Sammy and JAMMA filings for Japanese arcades; DFC Intelligence for PC; Newzoo, Sensor Tower and company filings from 2012 on; Newzoo by region, CNG/GPC for China and ISFE for Europe for the regional split.

      What is modelled here. Segment totals are researched; the split of those totals between individual platforms and between regions is estimation constrained to add up. Company-level regional mixes use affinity multipliers derived from published regional hardware splits — which is how the chart knows Xbox has no Japanese business. Eleven independently published regional figures are re-checked on every build and all currently agree within tolerance, most within 8%. Pre-1985 figures and arcade after 1990 are the least certain numbers on the chart.

      An earlier version offered a quarterly view. It was removed. No public quarterly series exists for this industry before roughly 2010, so every quarterly value was one seasonality assumption repeated fifty-seven times — a regular sawtooth that looked like a measurement and was not.
